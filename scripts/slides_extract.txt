What is a smart home?
02
A smart home is a living environment equipped with ambient sensors that continuously generate sensor sequences.
In our context:
Ambient sensors are non-intrusive sensors placed around the home that detect events such as movement, door openings, appliance usage and room presence.
These sensor readings can then be used to recognize everyday activities such as sleeping, eating, meal preparation or leaving the home.

===== SLIDE 12 =====
Human Activity Recognition
10
1. What is the problem
Not all activities are equally easy for a model to recognize.
Some activities produce distinctive sensor patterns → easier to recognize.
Other activities produce similar sensor events and occur in the same locations → their sensor patterns overlap.
As a result, the model may confuse one activity with another.
Different activities can look very similar from the sensors’ perspective.

===== SLIDE 14 =====
Complex Computing Problem
12
CCP Characteristic
How it applies to our FYP
No obvious solution / depth of analysis
No single classifier reliably solves HAR. We must compare feature representations, temporal models and classification approaches rather than apply a standard ML pipeline.
Depth of knowledge
Requires understanding of IoT sensor data, feature engineering, ML/DL, class imbalance, temporal dependencies, anomaly detection and evaluation.
Significant consequences
HAR errors propagate to anomaly detection, potentially causing missed deviations or false alerts. Evaluation must therefore consider false positives and false negatives, not accuracy alone.
Interdependence
The pipeline is dependent:
sensor data → preprocessing → HAR → contextual analysis → anomaly detection → alerts
. Errors in one stage affect later decisions.
Unfamiliar issues / beyond standard practice
Smart-home data is sparse, noisy, imbalanced and ambiguous. Activities may share sensors or overlap, making standard classification unreliable.

===== SLIDE 23 =====
Dei, ethics and legal consideration
19
DEI
Evaluate performance across different household and activity patterns.
Avoid assumptions about how users perform activities.
Make the system accessible and understandable to diverse users.
Check for unequal performance across different user/activity patterns.
Ethics
Protect the privacy of users and their daily routines.
Obtain informed consent for sensor-data collection and use.
Minimize the collection and retention of sensor data.
Clearly communicate system limitations and prediction errors.
Legal
Comply with applicable data-protection and privacy regulations.
Follow the dataset’s licensing and research-use requirements.
Securely store and anonymize/pseudonymize data where possible.
Avoid using predictions for high-stakes decisions without human oversight.
