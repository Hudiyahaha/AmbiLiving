**Routine deviation versus generic anomaly detection (RQ4).** The routine model detects
87–92 % of injected deviations on Aruba and 88 % on Milan when it is given annotated or
segment-recognised activities; the generic autoencoder detects 4–19 % <!-- C7 -->. The
baseline is also quieter, so we compare at matched false-alert rates: at no more false alerts
per clean day than the baseline's most sensitive setting, the routine model detects 56 % vs
18 % (Aruba, annotations), 55 % vs 25 % (Aruba, segment GB), 88 % vs 38 % (Milan, both) and
63 % vs 22 % (Milan, live) <!-- C7 -->. The gap is structural: the baseline detects **none** of
the missed meals or inactivity periods on any stream, because an outlier score over the episodes
that did occur cannot represent an episode that did not, or a silence <!-- C7 -->.

**Gradual drift.** Seven-day runs in which breakfast moves 20 minutes later per day are detected
in 2 of 7 runs on Aruba (annotations and segment streams), after a median of 5 days, and in
1 of 2 Milan runs only on the live stream <!-- C7b -->. Clean Aruba weeks raise 0.69–0.90 drift
alerts per day: the monitoring period (May–June) is compared with an observation period six
weeks earlier (March–April), so part of this is plausibly seasonal change.

**Error propagation (RQ5).** Replacing annotations with the segment recogniser (macro-F1 ≈ 85–90)
costs little: 92 → 87 % on Aruba, 88 → 88 % on Milan. Replacing it with the live window
recogniser (macro-F1 ≈ 43) costs a great deal on Aruba (87 → 16 %) and less on Milan (88 → 63 %)
<!-- C8 -->. Two mechanisms account for most of the Aruba loss (E14):

- *The learned routine is wrong, not just the day.* The live model rarely recognises Leave_Home,
  so in the observation period the hours the resident spends out are labelled as relaxing or
  working. The learned usual long quiet spell while relaxing becomes 188 minutes instead of 51,
  and a 2.5–4-hour injected silence no longer looks unusual (inactivity 93 → 11 %).
- *The sensors still suggest the activity.* Deleting every annotated cooking episode leaves
  Aruba's unannotated kitchen movement in the stream; the live model still predicts a median of
  six cooking episodes on those days (ten on clean days), so cooking never looks missed
  (missed 98 → 5 %).

Night-time toilet trips (inserted as whole sensor episodes) survive best (39 % on Aruba live).
Median detection delays with recognised activities are 0–5 minutes for unusual times, 17–35
minutes for prolonged activities, about 1–2 hours for inactivity and 2.5–3 hours for night toilet
trips (the count must build up); the missed-meal alert comes when the meal is about four hours
overdue on Aruba and 40 minutes on Milan.

**Part of the Aruba loss is episode building, not recognition.** The live stream above merges
runs shorter than three predictions (30 events). On Aruba this leaves many short fake episodes
(§5.2, Table T3b: precision 38 % against 54 % for a two-minute cleanup). We added the time-based
cleanup of §4.5 as a fourth stream, with *N* chosen on the observation days only (Aruba 3 minutes,
Milan 1 minute) and everything else unchanged. On Aruba, detection rises from 16 % to 45 % at the
same false-alert rate (1.30 → 1.34 per clean day): inactivity 11 → 86 %, night toilet trips
39 → 81 %, missed 5 → 21 %, prolonged 11 → 20 % and unusual time 13 → 17 %. The cleaned stream is
better at every point of the budget sweep, for example 53 against 27 % at about 2.4 false alerts
per day and 62 against 49 % at about 5. The jump in inactivity is consistent with the first
mechanism above; we have not yet measured how far the learned quiet-spell norm itself moves.
Deviations that depend on telling activities apart (a missed meal, an overlong or mistimed
activity) stay weak and need better recognition of those activities. On Milan, where the live
stream already worked, the cleanup does not help at matched false alerts (73 % at 5.0 false
alerts per day against 67 % at 4.1 for the original rule) <!-- C8c, E20 -->.

**False alerts and real change.** Clean-day alerts are 1.3 per day on Aruba and 2.5–3.1 per day
on Milan. Milan's monitoring days are the Christmas holiday: among the clean-day alerts on
annotations, 10 are activities that had not happened by their usual time (cooking late on 5
days, no leaving home by mid-afternoon on 3) and 10 are timeline changes — genuine departures from the routine of the two weeks before
<!-- C8b -->. These are counted as false alerts, which is conservative. §5.4 shows how routing
alerts by urgency turns this sensitivity into a manageable number of notifications.
